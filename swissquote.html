DESCRIPTION

As the lead designer on the Swissquote account at Dapper, I turned investing stories into LinkedIn carousels that make people stop, swipe and act.

THE CHALLENGE

While working at Dapper, a B2B marketing agency in Rotterdam, I became the main designer for Swissquote, one of Switzerland's leading online banks. The task was to turn complex investing topics into LinkedIn carousels and posts that people actually stop for. Every piece had to follow the brand guidelines, support the copy written by our content managers and push readers to one clear action: read an article, join a webinar or open an account.

CONTEXT

A SWISS BANK BUILT ONLINE

Swissquote is a Swiss online bank, headquartered in Gland and listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange since 2000. It gives traders and investors access to stocks, ETFs, crypto and banking in one place. Most of my work was for Swissquote Bank Europe, the group's bank in Luxembourg, speaking to investors who want their money to work harder.

PROCESS

COPY IN, STORY OUT

Every carousel started with three inputs: Swissquote's brand guidelines, the goal of the post and the copy from our content managers. My job was to build the visual story around that copy: what each slide shows, where the eye lands first and how the last slide turns attention into a click. Most carousels went through several feedback rounds before going live. This is how one cover slide comes together.

Anatomy of a cover

  1. The hook

    A question the reader answers in their head. The first half in Swissquote orange grabs attention, the second half in black lands the point.

  2. Breaking the frame

    The subject steps out of the photo, which adds depth and pulls the eye from the headline down into the image.

  3. Colour echo

    One orange detail in the photo ties the image back to the brand without adding a single extra graphic.

  4. Safe margins

    Text and image share the same 10% margin, so every slide in the series lines up when you swipe. Later I broke this rule on purpose.

CAMPAIGNS

ONE VOICE, MANY STORIES

Every topic was different, so the format had to feel familiar: a bold hook on the first slide, one idea per slide, an orange and light rhythm that keeps people swiping, and a clear call to action at the end.     PREMIUM

    GOING FULL BLEED

    At one point Swissquote wanted to brainstorm how its content could look more premium. My idea was to let the images go full bleed. Until then every photo sat inside a safe margin. Running images edge to edge made the slides instantly cleaner, calmer and more elegant: a look that speaks to the 1% of people with serious money to invest.

    CAMPAIGNS

    ONE MISSION THAT MATTERS

    When Andrew joined a team of 15 riders cycling from Barcelona to Paris to raise money for cancer treatment, the story already had real photos and real numbers. So I let both lead: full-bleed event photography, huge figures and as little text as possible. 3,333 km, 54,000 metres of climbing, 25 days in the saddle.

      MEASURING SUCCESS

      "Designing for a bank taught me that premium is a decision, not a decoration. The strongest slides were the ones where I removed something: a margin, a line of text, a second idea. Being trusted as the lead designer on a client this size pushed me to think like a partner, not just someone who executes."
